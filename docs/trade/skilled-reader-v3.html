Module 01 · Foundation
Context before signal
Every signal in your toolkit is structurally incomplete without the sequence that preceded it. The same pattern means different things in different locations. The retail error is to read the current candle. The skilled read begins several candles earlier.

The foundational basis: Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov (Journal of Financial Econometrics, 2014) demonstrated using NYSE TAQ data for 50 stocks that price changes over short intervals are primarily driven by order flow imbalance — the imbalance between supply and demand at the best bid and ask. The slope of this relationship is inversely proportional to market depth. This means the same OFI produces different price impact depending on where it occurs. Location is not a discretionary preference — it is mathematically embedded in how price responds to flow.

Was this a trending or balanced market?The same bearish candle means opposite things after 8 hours of selling into an HVN versus after a break of a 6-hour balance area. Without this you are reading a word without its sentence.
02 · LocationIs price moving into liquidity or away from it? Approaching HVN, VWAP, weekly level, prior POC?Location determines who is likely on the other side. New shorts below an HVN face a completely different absorption environment than new shorts entering open space below a broken balance area.
03 · During candleDid CVD confirm or diverge from price? Did OI expand or contract? High or low volume relative to recent bars?Only after establishing prior context does behavior during the candle become interpretable. Rising OI on a bearish candle means something specific only when you know what preceded it.
04 · Next candleDid price accept or reject the move? Did OI continue? Did CVD sustain or reverse?The candle you are analyzing is a hypothesis. The next candle is the first evidence that confirms or denies it. Most retail analysis stops at step 3 and never reaches step 4.

Module 02 · Foundation
OI as question, not answer
Open interest tells you that new contracts entered the market. It does not tell you which side initiated, whether they were right, or whether the move will continue. Every OI interpretation is a hypothesis about who opened — which prior context must either support or undermine.

The theoretical basis: Kyle's 1985 model (Econometrica) established that market makers observe only total order flow — they cannot distinguish informed from uninformed orders. This is the same problem you face reading OI. The model showed informed traders deliberately restrict trade size to camouflage their information. Visible, aggressive, large-OI moves are therefore less likely to be informed than passive, gradual, low-visibility accumulation.
What rising and falling OI actually tell you
Rising OI — the complete informational content
New contracts entered the market during this candle.
Four structurally different events produce identical rising OI:

1. Aggressive shorts entered — initiative sellers driving price lower with conviction
2. Longs absorbed — passive buyers accepting the other side of those shorts
3. Market makers hedged — delta-neutral positioning, no directional intent
4. Both sides expanded — balance auction, neither side dominant

Prior context, location, and CVD narrow down which is most probable. They never eliminate the ambiguity entirely.
Falling OI — the complete informational content
Existing contracts closed during this candle.
Four structurally different events produce identical falling OI:

1. Trapped shorts took profit — bullish for the move that trapped them
2. Trapped longs closed — bearish, capitulation, downward move had legs
3. Passive absorption unwound — the absorbing side exited after completing their position
4. Participation decay — both sides reducing, no strong conviction either way

A doji with falling OI after a bearish impulse is the canonical ambiguous case. All four readings are possible. Prior context and next candle are what resolve it.

Empirical note on hidden positioning: Bessembinder et al. found iceberg orders account for approximately 44% of order volume on major exchanges. Frey & Sandås (2009) showed iceberg orders on Xetra are on average 12–20× larger than visible limit orders. This means a significant fraction of the "passive absorption" case — longs absorbed on the other side of aggressive selling — is structurally invisible to OI until the position is complete. Falling OI on the doji may therefore reflect the absorbing side exiting after completing an invisible accumulation, not weakness.
What "new contracts entered" can mean — 4 candle sketches
Rising OI · Case 1
Aggressive shorts entered
Price falls sharply. CVD strongly negative. OI rising. New sellers are crossing the spread — initiative selling with conviction. The most straightforward read, but also the most commonly assumed without verification.
Rising OI · Case 2
Longs absorbed passively
Price falls but CVD is neutral or mildly negative. OI rises. A passive buyer is on the other side of those shorts — accepting fills at the bid without aggression. Identical OI to Case 1. Opposite implication.
Rising OI · Case 3
Market makers hedged
Price moves modestly. CVD mixed. OI rises in small increments consistently. Delta-neutral hedging — a market maker or options dealer creating offsetting inventory. No directional intent. OI reading is noise for directional analysis.
Rising OI · Case 4
Both sides expanded
Price barely moves. CVD oscillates around zero. OI rises steadily. Both sides are building positions simultaneously — a coiling balance auction. This is the pre-move state. Neither side is dominant yet.
What "existing contracts closed" can mean — 4 candle sketches
Falling OI · Case 1
Trapped shorts took profit
Price rises on a doji or small candle. CVD turns mildly positive. OI falls. Shorts that were trapped by a prior move up are buying back their positions. Bullish for the move that trapped them — not for new longs entering.
Falling OI · Case 2
Trapped longs closed
Price drops on a doji after a bearish impulse. CVD mildly negative. OI falls. Longs that were caught by the impulse are cutting their positions. Bearish confirmation — the downward move had enough force to capitulate the buyers.
Falling OI · Case 3
Passive absorption unwound
Price stabilises. CVD flat. OI drops sharply after a period of rising OI with flat price. The absorbing side has completed their position and removed their passive orders. Often immediately precedes a directional move.
Falling OI · Case 4
Participation decay
Price drifts. CVD near zero. OI falls slowly across multiple candles. Both sides are reducing — no conviction in either direction. The market is losing interest. Not a signal. A condition that requires waiting for new initiative.

On flow toxicity metrics (VPIN): Easley, López de Prado & O'Hara developed VPIN at Tudor Investment Corp as a real-time measure of order flow toxicity — distinguishing informed from uninformed flow using volume-synchronized buy/sell imbalance. However, subsequent independent research found VPIN's predictive power for short-run volatility is contested, and critics argue its apparent predictive content is partly mechanical. The underlying concept — that order flow imbalance contains information about who is trading — is empirically grounded. The specific implementation remains disputed. Use the concept; be skeptical of any single metric that claims to resolve the ambiguity mechanically.
Module 03 · Foundation
CVD ambiguity
CVD measures which side was aggressive — who crossed the spread. It does not measure who was right, who was institutional, or whether the move will continue. CVD is the most reactive signal in your toolkit, which makes it the most susceptible to misreading under time pressure.

What the research confirms: Cont et al. (2014) established a linear relationship between order flow imbalance and price changes, with the slope inversely proportional to market depth. This means CVD has genuine short-horizon predictive content — but only when market depth is consistent and the flow is not contaminated by spoofing, stops, or arbitrage. The five events below all produce identical CVD. Only one of them has continuation implications.
Five events that produce identical positive CVD
CVD positive — the complete informational content
More contracts traded at the ask than at the bid during this period.
1. Initiative longs entering — genuine directional conviction, new money, trend fuel
2. Short covering — not bullish conviction, positional unwinding with a ceiling
3. Stop-driven buying — shorts stopped out, mechanical flow that exhausts when stops clear
4. Spoof-induced buying — retail reacting to a manufactured DOM signal. Legally documented: the CFTC's enforcement action against Navinder Singh Sarao established that his layering algorithm placed large visible sell orders into the E-mini S&P order book, induced reactive buying from other participants, then cancelled before fill. The resulting CVD was entirely real — it came from genuinely executed market orders. The intent was manufactured.
5. Arbitrage execution — cross-venue or cross-instrument flow, no directional intent

Cases 1 and 2 produce opposite implications for continuation. Cases 3–5 reverse entirely when the trigger exhausts. CVD alone cannot distinguish between them.

Module 04 · Foundation
VWAP — honest limits
VWAP is a volume-weighted average price. It is the benchmark many institutional execution algorithms are measured against. That creates real behavioral consequences around it. But those consequences are regime-dependent, session-dependent, and event-dependent — and they break down completely in conditions you will regularly encounter.
Where VWAP has genuine meaning — and where it doesn't
The institutional basis — what makes VWAP real
Execution desks with VWAP mandates are measured on fill quality relative to session VWAP.
This creates real buy pressure below VWAP and sell pressure above it from this specific participant class. FINRA Rule 5310 requires broker-dealers to demonstrate best execution, and VWAP gives firms a concrete benchmark for regulatory compliance. Busseti & Boyd (Stanford, 2015) modelled optimal VWAP execution as a mean-variance minimisation problem — these algorithms are real, they dominate large-cap institutional order flow, and their benchmark creates measurable behavioral consequences around VWAP throughout the session.

The behavioral consequences are most reliable in liquid, high-volume, non-trending sessions with strong institutional participation. The key word is mandate — not all institutional flow has VWAP mandates. Prop desks, HFTs, and hedge funds will buy well above and sell well below VWAP when their thesis requires it.
Regime 1 — strong trend sessions
Price walks away from VWAP and does not return.
In a strong trend, VWAP becomes progressively less relevant. Price extended above VWAP in a bull trend is not "expensive" — it is where the market is going. Trading reversions to VWAP in a trending session means fading the dominant directional flow. The tell: VWAP slope is steep and price has not tested it in several hours.
Regime 2 — post-news event
The session VWAP is anchored to a pre-news distribution that no longer exists.
After a significant news event reprices the market, the session VWAP reflects the average of two different markets. Price "returning to VWAP" post-news is returning to an artifact. Switch immediately to a post-event anchored VWAP from the candle after the reprice.
Regime 3 — repeated VWAP tests
Each successive failed test changes the meaning of the next one.
First test — genuine decision point, two-sided. Second test — the defenders are still there, first-test buyers are underwater, probability of hold increases. Third test — the market is demonstrating that VWAP is a ceiling, not a fair value reference. Each failed test is not "building support" — it is evidence that one side is consistently winning. The fourth test often produces capitulation flush, not another rejection.

The habit this module builds: Before using VWAP as a reference, you identify the session regime — trending, ranging, or post-event. You note how many times VWAP has been tested and what each test produced. You verify the anchor is valid. Only then does VWAP enter your read.
Module 05 · Applied
Volume profile — the real read
Volume profile tells you where two-sided business was done and where one side dominated. HVNs and LVNs are not support and resistance — they are descriptions of past auction behavior that create probabilistic tendencies, not certainties. Understanding what they describe changes how you use them.
The foundational source: J. Peter Steidlmayer developed Market Profile at the Chicago Board of Trade in the 1980s, formalising observations from his time as a pit trader. The core principle — that price is an advertising mechanism that moves to find the other side, and that time/volume at a level measures acceptance — is practitioner theory from the exchange floor, later documented in Markets and Market Logic (1986) and expanded by James Dalton in Mind Over Markets (1993). Volume Profile is the electronic-era evolution of this framework, replacing time-at-price with contracts-transacted-at-price.
An HVN exists because buyers and sellers agreed to do large business at that price — a resolved disagreement where both sides transacted heavily. The probabilistic tendency: price returns to HVNs because both sides have inventory there and will defend or offload it when price revisits. An LVN exists because one side dominated completely — an unresolved auction where price moved through quickly because there was no opposing interest. Price moves through LVNs fast because there is no inventory to absorb momentum. These are tendencies, not rules — and they depend critically on whether the original participants who created those nodes are still present. A same-session HVN has live defenders. A 3-week-old HVN may be an empty shell.

Module 06 · Applied
Sequence reading
Sequence reading is the integration of all four tools across multiple candles simultaneously — not the identification of a pattern, but the construction of a conditional hypothesis with explicit invalidation conditions. A trade only exists when a hypothesis has been formed and the market has begun confirming it.
The canonical sequence — bearish impulse, doji, resolution

Building a conditional hypothesis — the structure
Hypothesis format — the internal monologue
Prior context establishes the frame: "Price has been selling for 6 candles into a known HVN. OI rising on sell candles — reactive positioning entering a structural level, not initiative selling below it."

Current candle raises the question: "Bearish impulse with high volume and negative CVD. Rising OI. Is this initiative selling or late shorts at the structural level where longs have inventory?"

Location narrows it: "We are at the HVN. Absorption is the competing hypothesis and it has structural support. The prior context — selling into the level — supports exhaustion over continuation."

The hypothesis: "This is likely late shorts entering into absorption. The doji and next candle will confirm or deny."
Confirmation: doji with falling OI, then recovery with positive CVD and no new OI expansion. Thesis holds. Entry after recovery candle closes.
Invalidation: next candle bearish with OI re-expanding and CVD strongly negative again. Initiative sellers are present at this level. Abandon the long hypothesis immediately.

Module 07 · Applied
When not to trade
The skilled reader's most important ability is recognizing when available information is structurally insufficient to form a high-probability hypothesis. Most frameworks teach entry. This module is about the conditions under which no framework produces reliable signal.
Condition 1 — Mechanical execution flow
Rhythmic, level-insensitive CVD with consistent increments regardless of structure.
A large institution executing a systematic order — rebalance, redemption, index reconstitution — produces CVD that does not respond to HVNs, VWAP, or structural levels. It sells through support at a consistent pace. Research basis: Harvey, Mazzoleni & Melone (NBER Working Paper 2025) on "The Unintended Consequences of Rebalancing" documented that mechanical rebalancing flows create predictable, level-insensitive order flow. The tell: consistent CVD increment size across multiple candles regardless of price level. No acceleration at LVNs, no deceleration at HVNs. This is not a directional trade — it is someone else's execution order. Wait for it to end, then trade the reversal.
Condition 2 — Pre-event window
Any signal in the 15–30 minutes before a known macro catalyst.
Order flow before a major event reflects positioning and hedging about the event — not the market's current fair value assessment. OI rising before a Fed announcement is not initiative positioning in the current market. After the event: re-anchor VWAP, re-assess the profile, wait for the first post-event OI reading to show where new initiative positioning is coming from.
Condition 3 — Genuine balance area
Price oscillating with two-sided OI, flat CVD, no structural bias.
In genuine balance, both sides are active and neither is dominant. Every signal your framework generates will be a false one. The setup is not inside the balance — it is the break from it. Watch for OI rising on one side of the range with CVD confirming, VWAP being abandoned in one direction, profile developing a skewed distribution. These precede the break. Trade the break, not the oscillation.
Condition 4 — Thin or illiquid conditions
OI and CVD signals on materially lower volume than the reference session.
In thin conditions, small orders move OI and CVD disproportionately. The institutional behavior that gives these signals meaning — VWAP mandates, structural HVN defense, systematic hedging — is largely absent. If current volume is below 30% of the reference session average, treat all OI and CVD signals as structurally unreliable until volume normalizes.

The habit this module builds: Before forming a hypothesis, you first ask whether the conditions exist for reliable signal formation. If you are in a pre-event window, thin session, mechanical flow environment, or genuine balance area — you are not looking for a trade. You are watching and waiting for the conditions that allow your framework to function.
Module 08 · Synthesis
The live process
What follows is not a checklist. It is a description of the internal process of a skilled reader in a live market — the actual sequence of questions, the conditional structure of the reasoning, and where a setup becomes a trade or doesn't. The goal is to make this process automatic through repetition, not to execute it consciously.
The internal process — before the session, early session, setup, confirmation
Before the session
Where did the prior session close relative to its POC and value area? Is overnight inventory net long or short relative to where fair value was established? What are the key structural levels price will encounter in the first hour? Where does this session open relative to VWAP, and what does that mean for the first directional test?

This is map-making, not signal-reading. The terrain is established before the first candle prints.
First 30 minutes
Is price accepting or rejecting the overnight range? Is OI expanding or contracting on early moves — initiative or responsive? Is CVD consistent with a directional bias or oscillating? Is the session trending, balanced, or reacting to an event?

No trades in most sessions until character is established. Trading before character means trading without context.
OI rising consistently in one direction with CVD confirming and price accepting: session is trending. Hypothesis formation adjusts — VWAP is less relevant, profile LVN breaks are more reliable.
OI oscillating with two-sided CVD: session is balanced. No directional trades until the balance resolves and the break is confirmed.
When a potential setup appears
The first question is not "what is this signal." The first question is "what preceded this, and where is price?"

Prior context first — what has OI been doing for the last 3–6 candles? Was the move into this level initiative or reactive? Does the current location change the meaning of the current signal?

Then the signal — what is OI doing? What is CVD doing relative to price? Are they telling the same story? If different, which has the higher claim in this context?

Then the hypothesis — what would have to be true for this signal to mean what it appears to mean? What do the next 1–2 candles need to do to confirm? What would invalidate it?
Hypothesis has a clean confirmation condition and prior context supports it: the setup exists. Wait for confirmation before entry.
Prior context is ambiguous, location is an obvious retail level, or signals require too many favorable assumptions: no trade. The setup does not exist yet.
At the confirmation candle
The confirmation candle answers the hypothesis. For an exhaustion read: does price recover, does OI stay flat or fall, does CVD turn positive? For continuation: does OI re-expand, does CVD sustain, does price accept the next level?

The confirmation candle is not the entry candle. It is the evidence candle. Entering during confirmation means entering before the evidence is complete. Entry is after confirmation — which gives up some of the move in exchange for probability. That is the correct trade.
Confirmation complete: entry. Stop defined by the invalidation condition — the price or OI/CVD behavior that means the hypothesis is wrong.
Partial or ambiguous confirmation: no entry. Reformulate with the new information. The market gave you more data — update the read, do not force the original thesis.
The three anchors — running through every read, every session
Anchor 1 — The interpretation structure
→
Prior context → location → behavior during → next candle. Always in that order.
Skipping prior context to read the current signal is the primary source of false reads. Every time you are tempted to act on a signal, ask first: what preceded this, and where is price? The answer to those two questions determines whether the signal means anything.
Anchor 2 — The cleaner framework
→
New positioning entered. Now ask five questions before assigning direction.
Was price moving into liquidity or away from it? Did CVD confirm or diverge? Did price gain acceptance? Did OI continue expanding? Did subsequent candles sponsor continuation? Without all five, the read is structurally incomplete. A label without answers to these questions is a retail shorthand, not an analysis.
Anchor 3 — Institutional reality
→
Before acting on any pattern, state what it most commonly represents in institutional terms — not what it looks like.
Sharp red candle + rising OI at an obvious level is most commonly late breakout shorts entering into liquidity — not smart money shorting. Doji + falling OI after that is most commonly short covering after liquidity extraction — not consolidation. The retail reading and the institutional reality are structurally opposed at exactly the locations where retail concentrates. That opposition is not accidental.
The complete picture — institutional reality across all patterns
What you seeRetail interpretationInstitutional reality
Clean setup at obvious levelHigh probability — level confirmedThe more obvious the level, the more retail is positioned there, the more useful it is as a liquidity source. Obvious levels are extraction points, not trade locations.
All signals align suddenlyMaximum confluence — enter nowAsk whether 3 of 4 were already aligned before the last candle. If not, you are watching one event generate four simultaneous lagging reflections — momentum cascade, not independent confirmation.
Conflicting, ambiguous signalsNo setup, move onConflicting signals at a structural level mean a genuine decision point. The highest-quality setups form here — but they require waiting for resolution, not acting on the conflict.

OI slope change before price movesUsually not noticedThe lead signal. Positioning changes before price reflects it. A skilled reader watches OI slope for inflection — not waiting for price to move and then reading OI to confirm it.
What separates the skilled reader: The framework reader identifies patterns and acts on them. The skilled reader builds conditional hypotheses from prior context, assigns explicit invalidation conditions, and acts only after the first candle of confirmation — then adjusts or abandons the hypothesis as new information arrives. The three anchors are the scaffold. Conditional reasoning is the structure. Screen time, records, and losses are what make it automatic.
What this framework is and is not: The OFI-price relationship (Cont et al.), the informed/uninformed trading problem (Kyle, Hasbrouck), the mechanics of spoofing (CFTC enforcement record), iceberg prevalence (Bessembinder, Frey & Sandås), and VWAP as execution benchmark (Busseti & Boyd, FINRA) are all empirically grounded. The interpretation hierarchy, the specific sequence logic for the doji + OI pattern, and the conditional hypothesis structure are practitioner reasoning consistent with that evidence — but not directly derivable from any single paper. The distinction matters: the foundational claims are verified. The framework built on top of them is a coherent inference, not a proven system. It requires your own screen time and records to calibrate against real markets.
